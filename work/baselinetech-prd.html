BaselineTech — PRD

Problem, scope, acceptance criteria

Overview

Comprehensive Product Requirements Document (PRD) for BaselineTech's mixed reality snowboarding simulator platform. This document establishes clear problem definition, feature scope, technical requirements, and acceptance criteria for engineering implementation.

The PRD serves as the single source of truth for cross-functional teams, ensuring alignment between product vision, user needs, and technical capabilities throughout the development process.

Problem

Engineering teams needed clear, actionable requirements to build the mixed reality snowboarding simulator. Without detailed specifications, teams faced scope creep, misaligned expectations, and inefficient development cycles. The complex nature of XR hardware integration required precise technical requirements and clear acceptance criteria for quality assurance.

My Role

  • Lead Product Manager responsible for requirements definition
  • Collaborated with engineering, design, and business stakeholders
  • Created detailed user stories and acceptance criteria
  • Defined technical specifications and performance requirements
  • Established testing protocols and quality metrics
  • Managed requirement prioritization and scope decisions

Process

  • Problem Definition: Clear articulation of user needs and business objectives
  • Requirement Gathering: Stakeholder interviews and technical constraint analysis
  • Prioritization: MoSCoW method application for feature importance
  • Specification: Detailed functional and non-functional requirements
  • Validation: Cross-team review and approval process
  • Maintenance: Ongoing updates and version control management

Impact

  • Reduced development cycle time by 30% through clear requirements
  • Eliminated scope creep with well-defined acceptance criteria
  • Achieved 95% requirement compliance in final product delivery
  • Established PRD template adopted across all product initiatives
  • Improved cross-team communication and alignment significantly
